👟 About the Challenge

Everything you need to know in one minute.

What is this?

A friendly step challenge for our team of 10, running September 1–30. Everyone logs their daily step count, and we race toward a shared team goal of 3,000,000 steps — that's about 10,000 steps each per day. There's an individual leaderboard too, if you're competitive about it. 😏

How to play

  1. Join once. Tap your name on the home page, or add yourself and pick an emoji. Your phone remembers you after that — no passwords, no accounts.
  2. Log your steps daily. Check your phone's step count, type the number, hit save. Takes about five seconds.
  3. Catch up on missed days. If you forget a day, the app shows you exactly which days you owe. Tap one and it jumps straight to that date so you can fill it in.
  4. Watch the board. The leaderboard and team progress update as everyone logs.

The rules

  • Only September 1–30 counts toward the leaderboard. Log before then if you want to practice — it won't count.
  • One entry per person per day. Saving the same day again just replaces the old number, so fixing a typo is easy.
  • Joining late? You're only ever shown as owing days from the date you joined onward. No guilt for days before you existed on the board.
  • It's the honor system. Nobody is auditing your pedometer. Be cool. 😉

Where do the steps come from?

Any step counter you like — your phone counts them in your pocket automatically, or use whatever your watch or fitness tracker reports. Just log one number for the day.

iPhone users can automate it. The home page has instructions for a Shortcuts automation that reads your Apple Health step count and posts it to the board every evening, so you never have to think about it again. Android doesn't offer an equivalent for health data, so Android folks log manually — it's two taps.

Note for WHOOP wearers: WHOOP tracks strain and recovery but doesn't count steps at all, so use your phone's count instead.

What data is stored?

Only two things: the name you type in and your daily step numbers. No email addresses, no passwords, no location, no health records, and nothing pulled from your phone without you sending it. The whole thing is a simple database table that gets thrown away after the challenge if we want.

Because there are no accounts, anyone with the link can view the board and log steps. That's on purpose — it keeps the whole thing to one tap. Don't put anything private in your display name.
